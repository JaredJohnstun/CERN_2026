S1 Sentence(s):
The signed fake factor weights are subtracting the simulated data that you don't want contributing to your results, so positive weights are for real data and negative weights are for simulated data. The Fake Factor is the ratio of ID yields to anti-ID yields because it yields a mapping of the antiID fake yields onto the ID region. In other words, it is a prediction of how many fake taus may end up in the ID region.

the softmask approach gives the data event a fractional weight representing how much it should contribute to the fake population.

The original problem: we want fake taus
Your collision data in the fake control region aren't pure jet→τ fakes. They contain the fake-τ contribution you want plus contamination from real/e-fake τ candidates.
In S1, you estimated the fake yield using ordinary signed subtraction:

Nfake​=Ndata​−NMC​

In terms of event weights, data contributes +cw and MC contributes −cw. That works for measuring yields, but it means you'd have negative event weights if you directly used the same approach to train the classifier.

What softmask subtraction does:
Instead, Shahzad's softmask method trains a separate subtraction classifier that estimates, for each data event, a quantity ρ(x) associated with the real/e-fake contamination. It then constructs:

w(softmask​)=cw[1−ρ(x)].   (cw) -> combined weight

So rather than saying:

DATA event → +weight
MC event   → -weight

you're effectively saying:

DATA event → keep some fraction of its weight,
             based on how much it looks like the
             fake population

MC event   → training weight = 0

The important part is that the resulting softmask_weight values are nonnegative. That's exactly what your output confirmed:
all weights >= 0: True
for all three folds.

We want soft/signed ​≈1
because that means the softmask subtraction reproduces the same estimated fake yield that the standard signed data−MC subtraction gives us.
The advantage is that softmask training uses nonnegative weights, avoiding the instability that negative event weights can cause in the training loss.
